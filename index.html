Common questions

North Carolina family law, in plain terms

Every case is different. These general answers can help you prepare for a conversation with an attorney.

How long do we have to be separated before filing for divorce in NC?

North Carolina generally requires spouses to live separate and apart for at least one year before filing for an absolute divorce, and at least one spouse must typically have lived in North Carolina for six months before filing. Other issues, such as custody, support, and property division, can often be addressed during the separation period.

How is child custody decided?

Courts generally decide custody based on what is in the best interest of the child. Many parents reach an agreement on their own or through mediation, which is commonly part of the process in contested custody cases. Existing custody orders can sometimes be modified when circumstances change.

What happens to our house and other property?

North Carolina uses “equitable distribution” to divide marital property and debt. Timing can matter — property claims generally need to be raised before an absolute divorce is finalized — so it’s wise to speak with an attorney early.

Do I need a separation agreement?

It isn’t required, but a written separation agreement can help spouses resolve issues like support and property without going to court. Because it is a binding contract, it’s important to understand the terms before signing.

What should I bring to my first consultation?

Helpful items include any court papers you’ve received, existing orders or agreements, a list of questions, and a general overview of income, assets, and debts. Not sure what applies to you? Call our office and we’ll let you know.
